The first experiment is to preserve the shape and timing of the spray that Go Fish already measures. The second is to identify the game's bite sound from the bundled samples before associating it with a splash. Both fit the current Go/Win32 capture pipeline and can be tested on a CPU with small buffers. Addon-free tracking addresses a separate problem, identifying which bobber belongs to this cast. The findings below come from the cited papers and the repository. The proposed fishing adaptations have yet to be benchmarked.

## What the current system sees

Bobber Marker identifies the player's bobber with brackets. Once it locks, the engine monitors the bobber at a nominal 30 Hz. The detector looks for nearly colourless pixels that brighten against a per-pixel exponential baseline. It compares the share of spray pixels in the centre disc against a floor and the rolling 90th percentile of recent spray. A rise in game-audio RMS usually confirms the splash. The repository documents a splash-to-sound delay of 200–450 ms in its recordings and an acceptance window that extends 800 ms after the last splash frame. Sound is mixed across players, so it can confirm a bite sound in the scene while the visual cue supplies location. ## Give the spray a short history

**Research.** Bobick and Davis condensed videos into maps of where motion occurred and how recently it occurred. Their experiments were on human movement. More recent work on precise event spotting shows the value of local frame-to-frame changes and longer temporal context for sparse events in sports video. The events and capture conditions in those benchmarks differ from fishing. [Bobick and Davis, TPAMI 2001](https://www.cs.bu.edu/faculty/betke/cs591/papers/bobick-davis.pdf) · [Hong et al., ECCV 2022](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136950033.pdf) · [Santra et al., CVPR 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00147)

**Fishing experiment.** Keep the existing positive, nearly colourless residual mask. The detector already measures centre concentration in the current frame for its silent fallback. Extend that idea by measuring spray fraction in a centre disc and an outer ring, when each region first rises, and whether the response is confined to one side. The hypothesis is that an own-bobber bite rises near the centre and sometimes spreads out, whereas recurring foam has a steadier pattern and nearby bites often enter from a side. The ring also gives a control for a flash or camera movement affecting the whole crop. Start with centre-versus-ring contrast and recent rise within the current detector crop. Test expansion and sector balance as additional features after checking whether real bites consistently show them. Timestamp the history in milliseconds, and count any frames spent waiting for a pattern as decision latency.

**If foam still dominates.** ViBe represents each pixel's background with recent samples from that pixel and its neighbours. That suggests a second comparison for pools where the same pixel repeatedly alternates between water and foam. Try a small history of typical residuals per region before adopting a full background model. Check recall on soft bites since learning recurrent bright states can also hide true spray. [Barnich and Van Droogenbroeck, TIP 2011](https://www.telecom.uliege.be/publi/publications/barnich/Barnich2011ViBe/index.html)

## Identify the sound and associate it with the right splash

**Research.** Baumgartner et al. detected transient whale calls by correlating a template with a spectrogram and thresholding correlation peaks. Synchformer learns audio–video offset from short segments and tests synchronization when matching cues are sparse. For Go Fish the useful ideas are to match a known sound against a mixture and test whether apparently plausible audio and video belong to the same event. [Baumgartner et al., JASA 2008](https://www.whoi.edu/cms/files/JAS001339_59390.pdf) · [Iashin et al., ICASSP 2024](https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/research/synchformer/)

**Fishing experiment.** Compare RMS onset against normalized correlation of a short, distinctive prefix of each bundled `BiteSoundPCM` sample. Start with PCM if real loopback examples retain waveform alignment. Search only the recent audio around a visual candidate, and compare downsampled audio if scanning every lag is too expensive. If mixing, playback processing, or pitch shifts spoil waveform matching, compare short time–frequency templates. Evaluate several prefix lengths against game audio, since a long match can improve identification while delaying the decision. A matcher can estimate onset retrospectively, so log both the estimated sound onset and the time enough samples were available to act. [Bundled assets and decoding](../../../assets/embed.go)

Keep vision as the ownership cue. Compare the current timing gate with a lag score that favours the measured delay range while allowing capture jitter. Include negatives with genuine bite audio from a neighbour, authentic game sound shifted within or across casts, and multiple plausible splash candidates in the same window. These are harder than silence or random noise because the sound itself can match perfectly. Score cases with unavailable audio, muted effects, and quiet but working audio separately, and measure whether stronger visual evidence can support the visual fallback in those cases.

## Use the cast to find an unmarked bobber

**Research.** ByteTrack uses weak detections to extend an established track when they agree with its motion. TAPNext++ is a recent frame-by-frame point tracker that explicitly evaluates re-detection after occlusion or an exit from the frame. These papers offer different comparisons for continuation and reacquisition after a missed observation. [Zhang et al., ECCV 2022](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136820001.pdf) · [Jung et al., CVPR Findings 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10582)

**Fishing experiment.** Compare the repo's static normalized cross-correlation locator against one that searches after the known cast time and ranks newly appearing candidates by their persistence near the landing position. Use local NCC for follow-up updates. A lower NCC score can continue a consistent track for a short gap, while establishing a new identity needs stronger evidence. Test crowded water and casts where the first visible bobber belongs to someone else. Compare an offline run of TAPNext++ seeded by a manually identified landing point only if local tracking loses a correctly seeded bobber. Its seeded result measures continuation, while discovering and identifying the landing still requires a separate evaluation. [Existing locator](../../../domain/detect/ncc_locator.go)

The bracket can supply approximate position labels for developing this locator, as artificial fiducials do for ground truth in other vision systems. Keep bracket-derived coordinates in the labels and keep the brackets and outcome signal out of detector inputs. Collect unmarked, uncropped sequences for evaluation, including time before the cast and after landing. Painted-over brackets or marker-centred black padding can still reveal position. Treat the addon outcome as a separate label for the reel result, and validate precise bite times from the video. [Olson, ICRA 2011](https://april.eecs.umich.edu/pdfs/olson2011a.pdf) · [Recording format](../reference/datasets.md)

## Replay that can answer the question

1. **Annotate independently.** Keep the existing recordings for coarse screening. `-label` estimates bite time from a fixed human reaction delay. `-label-bot` estimates it from the incumbent detector and excludes casts without observed reels or outcomes. Both can bias a latency comparison. Hand-label a held-out set with the last clearly pre-bite frame and first clearly visible bite frame, plus genuine no-bite casts and timeouts. Record a small native-rate set with synchronized audio. At roughly 10 fps, the existing recording has about 100 ms between frames even before gaps, and rarely records the whole window, which limits both timing and addon-free tracking comparisons. [Dataset and label details](../reference/datasets.md)
2. **Isolate the changes.** Replay the baseline, the visual-history change alone, audio matching alone, and both together, at the recorded timestamps with no future frames. Use session-level splits and hold out water types or fishing spots where practical. Report open water, pools, crowded water, and audio modes separately. Include landing ripples, adjacent bites, spells, camera shifts and quiet effects. Run the simulator for deterministic checks on timing and missing audio. Its scripted bright splash and bundled sound are controlled inputs, so discrimination on real water and sound mixtures needs real footage. [Simulator](../tutorials/simulator.md)
3. **Measure what matters to the reel.** Compare first-trigger recall at the incumbent's false-trigger rate, false triggers per cast, and p50/p95 delay to the actual decision. Report early triggers separately and use annotation intervals to expose uncertainty. Keep the existing broad `detecteval` acceptance window for continuity and add tighter, predeclared timing tolerances for the native-rate clips. Benchmark processing time and missed frame ticks on Windows alongside detection quality. For the addon-free branch, also report time to first correct lock, wrong-bobber locks per cast, and recovery after occlusion. Replay locator, detector and FSM together for an end-to-end check after evaluating them separately, since `detecteval` gives all bite detectors common watched areas. [Evaluator](../../../cmd/detecteval/eval.go) · [Precise spotting evaluation](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136950033.pdf)

For broader game automation, Minecraft Video PreTraining used an inverse dynamics model to turn unlabelled video into action-labelled training data. The analogous asset here is instrumented play that yields location and outcome labels cheaply, with a small, independently annotated test set. The fishing state machine already defines what to do once a bite is spotted, so a learned gameplay policy has lower priority than measuring the detector. [Baker et al., NeurIPS 2022](https://cdn.openai.com/vpt/Paper.pdf)
